# Re-orient a scan taken at an angle (2026-11-05)

## Why
Parts are scanned leaning on a block (the CT holder), so the scan's axes are not the part's. Depth below the top face,
the long axis, wall thickness through the slab and every section view only mean what they mean on the bench once the
part is level. Matt's choice (poll, 11-05): **auto-level, then nudge**.

## What the user does
1. Opens a scan and segments it as usual (the auto-level needs the sample mask).
2. In **Scan information > Orientation**, presses **Level the part**. VoxelBench finds the part's axes from its sample
   mask: long axis to x, thinnest direction (the slab normal) to z, the third to y.
3. The three views now show the scan in that frame (a live preview: only the shown slices are resampled).
4. Fine-tunes with three angle sliders (tilt about x, tilt about y, turn about z, ±45°, 0.5° steps), **Flip over**
   (180° about x) and **Turn 90°** (about z), watching the slices. A part's top cannot be told from its bottom by
   shape alone, so the flip is the user's call.
5. Presses **Apply**. The scan is rebuilt in the new frame, the analysis restarts, and the orientation is saved with
   the sample. Reopening the scan applies it again. **Back to scan axes** undoes it.

## Decisions (my calls unless marked)
- **Nearest-neighbor resampling.** Every gray value in the leveled scan is a measured voxel, so thresholds, the
  histogram and the density calibration keep their meaning; positions move at most half a voxel. Trilinear would
  smooth the noise and shift porosity at a fixed threshold.
- **The leveled grid holds the part, not the whole scan:** the part's box in the new frame plus 2 mm of air on every
  side, at the loaded voxel size. This keeps the copy small enough to build while the original is still in memory.
- **Outside the scan is gray 0.** Voxels that map outside the original volume or its reconstruction circle are written
  as 0; measured voxels that read exactly 0 become 1. The leveled scan has no circle (it is not a z-cylinder any
  more), and the existing rule "a block holding any 0 is outside" then marks the outside exactly.
- **Memory check before building:** the original plus the leveled copy must fit in 1.5 × the load budget (1.8 GB in
  Chrome, 0.9 GB in Safari). Reopening a leveled scan picks the binning so that both fit.
- **Marks stay with their frame.** Regions, crops, annotations, rejections and saved runs record the orientation they
  were made in. Marks from another frame are hidden, with a note saying how many; they come back under that frame.
- **Ambiguous axes are flagged.** When two of the part's extents are within 10 % (a cube), the auto-level says which
  axes are uncertain, and the sliders settle it.
- The rotation is stored as a row-major 3 × 3 matrix (rows = the part's x, y, z in scan coordinates), the grid's
  origin in scan voxels and its size, plus the slider angles, in the sample record (`orientation`).

## Not in this round
- The command-line runner (`tools/headless_run.js`), the scipy reference and the Dragonfly kit work on scan axes only.
  The Dragonfly panel asks for **Back to scan axes** first.
- The zoomed full-resolution XY view (binned scans) is off for a leveled scan.
- The CAD comparison and filler step lose their circle-edge rules on a leveled scan (outside is gray 0 instead).

## Plan (red-green per item)
1. `app/vb_orient.js` (pure, `tests/orient.test.js`):
   - `levelFromMask(mask, dims)`: centroid, principal axes, right-handed R, `ambiguous` flags.
   - `nudge(R, {rx, ry, rz})`, `flip(R)`, `turn(R)`: rotations in the part's frame; det(R) stays +1.
   - `gridFor(R, mask, dims, bin, marginVox)`: origin and size of the leveled grid around the part.
   - `resample(slices, dims, circle, orient)`: nearest neighbor, outside 0, measured 0 to 1, slice by slice.
   - `samplePlane(slices, dims, circle, orient, plane, index)`: one leveled plane for the preview.
   - `orientId(orient)`, `sameFrame(record, id)`, `fitsMemory(...)` / `planOrientedBinning(...)`.
2. Worker: a `partMask` message returning the binned sample mask.
3. Wiring: the Orientation block, the preview in the views, Apply / Back to scan axes, the load-time rebuild,
   frame tags on new marks and runs, the hidden-marks note, captions "Part x" when leveled.
4. Browser check on a tilted synthetic part and on Sample 2; push; republish the artifact.

## Result (2026-11-05)
- Built as planned: `app/vb_orient.js` (14 tests in `tests/orient.test.js`), `app/vb_orientrail.js`, the `partMask`
  worker request, and the load-time rebuild in `ct_app.js`.
- Synthetic tilted slab in the browser (12 × 6 × 2 mm, turned 25°, −10°, 30°): axes within 0.12° of the truth; the
  leveled grid is the slab plus 2 mm; four pores found tilted and leveled, the leveled sizes closer to the truth.
- On a real lab scan, leveling changed the total porosity through the OPEN porosity only (closed porosity within a few
  percent). Cause: the sample envelope is a box closing (`ct_pores.js` buildBody, `boxDilate`/`boxErode`), which reaches
  farther along the box diagonals, so open porosity depends on how the part sits in the voxel grid, leveled or not.
  A spherical closing would remove that; it changes the segmentation, so it is a separate decision.
