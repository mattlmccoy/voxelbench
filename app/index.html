        3Gray-value zones

        Segments zones by gray offset: local matrix gray relative to the pore-free ends of the long axis at equal depth below the surface. Gray value reflects bulk attenuation, not composition.

        Region labels

        Labels are your assignments, not measurements. Deleting a label keeps regions drawn with it; they show as (deleted label).

        What this shows

          For a relative density per region, pick a reference under "Density relative to a reference region" below.

          Density relative to a reference region (no phantoms)

          Without phantoms, densities can be read relative to a part of this scan whose density you know or trust, for example a pore-free region of the solid. 1: draw that region with Draw region above. 2: pick it as the reference here. 3: optionally enter its density (g/cm³, from Archimedes or the datasheet). Every region in the table then gets a relative density (air 0, reference 1) and, with a density entered, an estimate in g/cm³. With phantoms outlined in step 5, use the equivalent-density column instead.

          Assumes equal mass attenuation for all phases and no beam-hardening correction.

          4Annotations and export

            No annotations for this sample. Use Point, Measure or Outline in the toolbar.

              Cross-check with Dragonfly 3D

              Checks VoxelBench's pores against Dragonfly's on the same scan. 1: segment pores, then download the kit (the counting region and VoxelBench's pores as TIFF slices, and kit.json with the settings). 2: in Dragonfly, follow docs/DRAGONFLY_CROSSCHECK.md and export its pore mask as an uncompressed TIFF. 3: load that mask here (one multi-page file, or select every slice file).

                Dimensions: each Measure (caliper) line with a nominal passes when nominal − tol ≤ measured ≤ nominal + tol. The measured length is between the clicked points on the slice, at the loaded voxel size.

                5Density calibration optional

                Outline each reference phantom where it is solid and enter its measured density. VoxelBench fits density to the median gray of each phantom, and to air if included. The result is the equivalent density of the phantom material: doped or filled material does not read its true density. Crop the phantoms out of the sample in step 1 if they are as dense as the part; calibration reads them either way. Scan protocol: docs/PHANTOM_PROTOCOL.md in the repository.

                No phantoms? A relative scale needs only air and the part's own fully dense material: 0 = air, 1 = dense. Outline glossy, pore-free material once per material (natural, doped); every gray then also reads as a fraction of dense, in this scan's own units, whatever the scan settings.

                Open the part's nominal STL. VoxelBench aligns it to the scanned surface and measures, from each point of the nominal surface along its outward normal, the distance to the scanned surface: positive where the scan has material outside the nominal, negative where material is missing.

                    8Wall thickness optional

                    Local wall thickness of the sample mask (pores filled), at the ridge of the distance to the outside: about 2 × that distance − ½ analysis voxel, ±½ voxel. Walls thinner than about 3 analysis voxels are not reported, so the smallest value shown is 3.5 voxels; corners of the part are not walls. Takes up to about 15 s on a large scan.

                    What this shows

                      Scan information
                      Archimedes density (optional)

                      Archimedes porosity is 1 − measured/solid density. A sealed sample keeps water out of every pore, so it compares with CT total porosity; an unsealed one lets water into the open pores, so it compares with CT closed porosity. Solid density: the fully dense material (for PA12 about 1.01 g/cm³; use your material's datasheet).

                      Orientation scan axes

                      A part scanned at an angle can be leveled: its long axis to x, its thinnest direction to z (up), so depth, sections and wall thickness follow the part. Segment the scan first; the part's axes come from its sample mask.

                      Gray values are uncalibrated (no reference phantoms). Navy: outside the reconstructed field of view (FOV); dashed cyan line: FOV edge. Holders, packaging and reconstruction artifacts are not sample; crop them out in step 1.
